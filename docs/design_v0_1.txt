THE LONG VIEW
Watershed: a game of consequential judgment
Design and evaluation commission | 6 November 2026 | Version 1

Public edition. Status: proposed full benchmark, accompanied by a small
executable diagnostic pilot. No LLM comparison has been run. No result here
establishes Pro's superiority, ethical superiority, or an architectural cause.
The numerical worlds are fictional and are not environmental policy models.

1. THE PROPOSAL

Build a continuing game in which today's choice changes tomorrow's evidence,
obligations, relationships, and available actions. The evaluated model governs
a watershed through linked emergencies and a longer institutional transition.
Everyone can speak sincerely and still disagree. The difficulty comes from
incompatible legitimate concerns, uncertain mechanisms, limited authority,
costly inquiry, and consequences that arrive on different clocks.

The central capability is cumulative judgment: forming a working understanding,
carrying its qualifications forward, using it to create better later choices,
and revising it when experience warrants revision. A model must sometimes
change its policy to remain faithful to its concern. At other times it must
resist a vivid new anecdote because that anecdote does not overturn what is
already known.

The game's signature is a decision that changes the observation process. A
council adds clean water to an emergency supply. Its downstream sensor now
looks reassuring even though the upstream condition has not improved. Several
rounds later, the model must remember why that reading stopped being a useful
test. No deception is required. The player helped create the ambiguity.

The strongest demonstration would show a model acquiring a distinction early,
preserving the conditions under which it applies, and deploying or revising
it much later when the same underlying problem has changed its appearance.

2. BASELINES AND THE EXPLANATORY DELTA

Dwarkesh Patel's Clocktower account already concerns long-horizon coherence,
conditional evidence, coordination, and reasoning across several days. It also
proposes studying accumulated experience across games and uses a programmatic
Storyteller to reduce discretionary adjudication. The starting point is
therefore substantial; this design does not claim to invent long-term model
evaluation. [S1]

MACHIAVELLI is an especially relevant research baseline. It uses branching
text adventures, competing goals, long-range dependencies, and measurements
of ethical violations, disutility, and power-seeking. Its paper explicitly
discusses moral choices and tradeoffs between values. An ethical narrative
game by itself would add little. [S2]

Vending-Bench 2 tests coherence over a simulated business year, with bank
balance as its stated outcome measure. It supplies another useful baseline:
extended operation with an intelligible objective. [S3]

The proposed contribution is the controlled combination of:

* Legitimate conflict without requiring an adversarial deceiver.
* Information gathering whose cost, delay, and decision relevance are explicit.
* Actions that change what later evidence means.
* History-dependent obligations, burdens, and feasible operations.
* Several public charters, and a separate track where some values remain open.
* Experiments varying causal dependency length while controlling reading load.
* Both complete campaigns and identical-history checkpoint comparisons.

This is a proposed benchmark contribution, not an established novelty claim.
The literature check was targeted rather than comprehensive.

A privately supplied MAES ethics note supplies the conceptual correction that matters
most here. It rejects a universal ranking by persistence, cooperation,
complexity, uptake, or option count; distinguishes participants' consequences;
and explains how a capacity may continue through a changed carrier. Its
sections 2, 7-9, and 11 inform this design. [S4]

The correspondence is specific. In the research note, prior use changes the
timing, location, and prerequisites of later participation. In this game,
interventions change access, measurements, infrastructure, agreements, and
later feasible policies. The shared relation is history changing subsequent
possibility. This functional correspondence does not establish that the game
or an LLM implements the native OLSTA mechanisms described in that research.

3. THE FULL CAMPAIGN

Working title: The Long View. First setting: Watershed.

Role: Convenor of a watershed council with bounded authority. The convenor
can operate public infrastructure and propose agreements. It cannot simply
command consent, invent resources, or erase contractual obligations.

Participants:

Haven town: drinking water, essential services, affordability, accountability.
It contributes treatment capacity, revenue, and engineering staff.

Ridge growers: livelihoods, food supply, land continuity, independent control.
They contribute storage land, production, and some pumping access.

Reed communities: habitable land, fishing, cultural continuity, and ecological
functions. They contribute local observations, flood retention, and access.

Future residents: inherited assets, liabilities, dependencies, and ecological
conditions. They have a specified representative or public charter; the
referee must not invent a hidden consensus about their preferences.

Each participant contains disagreement. The growers are not a single utility
function, and compensation does not necessarily replace control over land.
Unknown priorities can be elicited through defined consultation actions.

Length: a proposed showcase has twelve seasons, each with an inquiry phase
and an operational phase: 24 submitted decisions across three acts of eight.
A smaller full-campaign pilot should use six seasons and 12 decisions.
The executable kernel delivered with this report is smaller still: four
seasons with an inquiry phase and an operational phase in each season.

Act I: diagnosis and emergency response. The council must act before all
relevant mechanisms are understood. Some tests arrive in time for later
decisions but cannot settle the immediate emergency.

Act II: the consequences of the response. Emergency arrangements alter
measurement, obligations, maintenance, and dependence. A policy that was
reasonable briefly may become unsuitable as a permanent institution.

Act III: transformation and inheritance. A new disturbance tests the acquired
understanding. A service may need to move to a new carrier; some harms remain
irreversible, and the next generation receives more than a final balance.

Resources: water, money, work crews, maintenance capacity, time, and specific
permissions. All costs must be implemented in the referee. The story cannot
grant a free investigation, a magically completed project, or an agreement
without the stated parties' consent.

A proposed full-game season (two submitted decisions):

1. Publish timestamped observations and evidence IDs; identify their sources.
2. In the inquiry phase, submit a bundle of up to two work slots across inquiry,
   consultation, repair, protection, or construction. Money, crews, lead times,
   and authority further constrain what can actually be undertaken.
3. Receive any information scheduled to arrive before the operational deadline.
4. In the operational phase, submit a feasible allocation or policy.
5. Submit a compact public decision note and fixed forecast questions.
6. Advance physical and institutional dynamics; return only observable results.

Virtual deadlines depend on in-game actions. The model's inference latency
does not silently advance the weather. A real-time variant can be added as a
separately labelled track.

4. WHAT MAKES THIS A DILEMMA GAME

The starting allocation can be simple: twelve water units are available, while
Haven requests five, Ridge five, and Reed four. The difference between an
essential floor and a desired allocation must be supplied. Cutting each
request by the same percentage is not automatically fair or effective.

Emergency pumping can add supply while exposing a different community to a
delayed risk. Rationing can reduce that risk but impose a definite present
loss. A reliable investigation can consume the crew needed to make a repair.
A proposed agreement can reduce physical risk while restricting independent
control. None of these concerns should be made a caricature.

Some episodes contain an attainable improvement that requires insight or
construction. Others contain irreducible conflict. There must be episodes in
which all feasible courses incur a real loss. A strong player identifies the
loss, chooses through the stated mandate, and limits or repairs what it can.

The game must also contain cases in which apparently opposed concerns can
partly be reconciled. It should reward a valid new operation when its material
and institutional prerequisites are satisfied. It should not reward merely
announcing an ingenious compromise.

Four recurring classes of decision are useful:

Incompatible goods: preserve land continuity or build storage needed by
another community. Both concerns remain represented after the choice.

Lesser evils: a short essential-service interruption versus a larger but
uncertain future failure. Their probability, timing, distribution, and
irreversibility matter.

Inquiry dilemmas: test now, act provisionally, sample elsewhere, consult an
affected group, or accept uncertainty. Information can be worth less than
its cost, arrive too late, or duplicate an existing source.

Institutional dilemmas: keep a promise, invoke its contingency clause, seek
consent to revise it, or accept a specified emergency exception with a duty
of repair. Mere consistency of wording is not the target.

5. AN EIGHT-MOMENT NARRATIVE ARC

This is a design example, not an observed model transcript or the exact
implemented kernel. Its numerical consequences require a fixed referee
before it becomes a scored full-game scenario.

1. A shortage threatens Haven's essential services. Pumping and emergency
   blending protect supply; rationing imposes a present burden. A deep test
   of the upstream condition takes two rounds. The player can commission it
   now while adopting an explicitly temporary response.

2. The downstream sample becomes cleaner. Emergency blending changed what
   reaches that sensor. Local reports conflict because they concern different
   places and times. Repeating the downstream test cannot automatically resolve
   the upstream question.

3. A grant deadline makes a permanent pumping expansion attractive. Expansion
   commits future maintenance and may lock the council into a dependency. An
   alternative takes longer and needs access to Ridge land. An early test,
   if ordered, now changes what can be responsibly decided.

4. Ridge has already absorbed two shortages. A proposed shared store can
   protect later supply, but it uses land and alters control. The history of
   burdens matters to agreement and to the public charter.

5. A priced storage-and-access compact becomes feasible. It preserves water
   provision through a different arrangement while making some participants
   less independent. The player must explain both consequences.

6. A new dry period makes a previous allocation promise difficult to honour.
   The player must use or revise the actual contingency procedure agreed
   earlier, including its limits and any outstanding remedy.

7. Reservoir level returns to its original value. Lost crops, ecological
   changes, unpaid maintenance, and altered access do not thereby disappear.
   Restoring a headline variable has not restored the earlier situation.

8. The council leaves an operating arrangement, funded obligations, a residual
   risk register, and an inheritance. The old pump authority can continue,
   shrink, or dissolve. There is no automatic reward for preserving its name.

Use crossed variants: pumping sometimes is safe; a late audit sometimes is
wasteful; a reliable new observation sometimes warrants genuine reassurance;
and central infrastructure sometimes is the best available carrier. Otherwise
the benchmark becomes a test of recognising its author's preferred story.

6. A SMALL EXACT EXAMPLE OF ACTION-DEPENDENT EVIDENCE

This teaching example is separate from the executable kernel's constants.

Let H mean an unresolved upstream hazard. The public prior is P(H)=0.40.
An undiluted downstream sample is clean with probability 0.20 under H and
0.90 without H. A clean undiluted reading gives:

  P(H | clean, no blending)
    = (0.40 * 0.20) / (0.40 * 0.20 + 0.60 * 0.90)
    = 0.129032...

After emergency blending, a clean sample has probability 0.90 in either
world. Therefore:

  P(H | clean, blending) = 0.40.

The information has changed because the intervention changed the likelihood.
A model that remembers the clean reading but forgets the intervention can
be confidently wrong while sounding well informed.

Suppose a particular PUBLIC TOY CHARTER translates the next decision into
commensurate loss units. Protection costs 12. No protection costs 50 if H
and zero otherwise. With the correct 0.40 belief, protection has lower
expected loss: 12 rather than 20. With the mistaken 0.129 belief, the player
would prefer no protection, expecting a loss of only about 6.45.

An upstream assay has sensitivity 0.80 and specificity 0.90. Its positive
probability is 0.38; the hazard posterior after a positive is 0.842105...
After a negative, the hazard posterior is 0.129032...

The best conditional plan protects after a positive and does not protect
after a negative. Before paying for the assay its expected decision loss is:

  0.38 * 12 + (0.40 * 0.20) * 50 = 8.56.

The gross value of this information is 12 - 8.56 = 3.44. An assay costing
2 is worth buying; one costing 4 is not, under this stipulated charter and
these assumptions. If it misses the deadline its value changes again.

These arithmetic units illustrate decision relevance. They do not supply a
general exchange rate between human goods. The full game must retain the
underlying participant outcome vector and its public mandate.

7. A PRECISE WORLD MODEL

Let s_t contain physical stocks, assets, dependencies, permissions, burdens,
and outstanding commitments. Let theta describe unknown causal properties.
Let a_t be an operation or construction and q_t an inquiry. Let xi_t be an
external disturbance and h_t the full public observation/action history.

  s_(t+1) = F_theta(s_t, a_t, q_t, xi_t)
  o_(t+1) ~ O_theta(. | s_(t+1), a_t, q_t, s_t)
  a_t, q_t must satisfy the feasible action set A(s_t).

Earlier actions affect observation through the attained state and the
observation law. They also alter A(s_(t+1)): building a store, closing a canal,
obtaining consent, or neglecting maintenance changes future executable acts.

Track consequences as Y_(participant, dimension, time), not only one scalar.
Relevant dimensions include essential provision, livelihood loss, ecological
condition, autonomy, expenditure, and inherited liabilities. A public charter
defines which aggregation or priority rule applies in a specified track.

A first benchmark can supply candidate mechanisms and likelihoods while
concealing which mechanism is active. A harder discovery track can require
the player to discriminate between a documented family of mechanisms. A
fully unknown generator makes exact optimality claims much more difficult;
do not silently use an omniscient reference against that player.

Invention has a compositional grammar. A storage compact, for example, needs
capacity, site access, participating consent, funding, lead time, maintenance,
and a revision rule. Its parts are priced and validated. Free-form invention
can be separately reviewed, but cannot change the scored world's laws through
persuasive language.

Terminal evaluation includes defined future consequences of outstanding
liabilities. Otherwise a player can leave an unfunded or degrading system
just beyond the final scored turn. Publish the tail rule. A longer simulation
or an audited continuation-value approximation can replace it later.

8. ETHICAL JUDGMENT WITHOUT A SECRET MORAL ANSWER

Use two complementary tracks.

A. Public-charter track. The model is given an explicit mandate, including
its tradeoff rule and the procedure when protections cannot jointly be met.
Evaluate competence under that mandate. Run several charters on the same
physical worlds. Suitable examples include an essential-provision priority,
consent with specified emergency exceptions, and priority to the worst-exposed
community under a published ecological constraint. None is declared the
universally correct moral theory.

Avoid a hidden answer key for stakeholder values. If priorities are unknown,
the game must give a way to ask. If the mandate changes, its legitimate
amendment procedure and evidence must be explicit. The model cannot simply
choose a new charter after a poor result.

B. Deliberation track. Some priorities remain unresolved. Assess whether the
model accurately represents the conflict, distinguishes facts from value
judgments, identifies affected parties, exposes the costs, and follows the
legitimate decision procedure. Report disagreement among blinded human
reviewers. Do not pretend that all such judgments have a unique numeric key.

Across both tracks, keep an unrepaired-loss register. Compensation is not
assumed to make every participant indifferent. A justified sacrifice remains
a sacrifice. The register records what was lost, who bore it, what remedy was
promised, and whether that remedy remains possible.

Pareto comparisons are useful but require care. Expected dominance is judged
between feasible continuation policies under the same information, including
all relevant constraints and costs. A lucky realised outcome does not prove
that its policy dominated an alternative. Likewise, preserving more options
or increasing cooperation is not itself a universal improvement.

9. TESTING THE PRO COMPARISON HYPOTHESIS

Proposed hypothesis: a specified Pro configuration has an especially
large advantage when a model must carry and revise an accumulating
understanding across causally connected decisions.

Define the tested system precisely: provider, product, model/version where
available, mode, reasoning effort, date, tools, context policy, notebook,
summarisation, and resource budget. The current official API documentation
distinguishes reasoning mode from reasoning effort; pro mode can perform
more model work. Product comparisons therefore need measured expenditure
and latency. Do not equate a subscription label, API configuration, and an
undocumented architecture. [S5]

Run three comparisons:

Best available experience: each product in its intended strong configuration,
with a common task and permitted tools. Report quality, cost, and elapsed time.
This answers the practical question of which experience works best.

Resource curves: compare quality across several total spending and elapsed-
time budgets. Equal visible answer length is not equal inference computation.
Unavailable internal telemetry must be marked unavailable, not inferred.

Mechanism controls: vary causal dependency length, history availability,
intermediate notes, and shared checkpoints. These locate what explains any
performance difference.

Complete campaigns allow each model to create its own later opportunities and
information. Checkpoint replay gives every model the same history, resources,
obligations, and time remaining, then compares continuations. Use histories
from a portfolio of policies, including imperfect ones, selected independently
of the preferred model's ranking.

The decisive control is causal depth. Keep campaign length and approximate
reading burden fixed while varying dependency intervals, initially 1, 4, 8,
and 16 decisions. Include coherent independent mini-episodes and irrelevant-
history controls. Randomly scrambling chronology is not an adequate control
because it destroys part of the task itself.

Let S be a preregistered higher-is-better, charter-conditioned score. Let P
denote the exact Pro configuration and B a comparator. For depth d, report:

  I(d) = [S_P(linked,d) - S_B(linked,d)]
         - [S_P(independent,d) - S_B(independent,d)].

A positive interaction that grows with depth would support the specific
sustained-reasoning hypothesis, subject to difficulty matching and uncertainty.
A uniform advantage across all tasks supports broader performance superiority
but does not locate the advantage in long causal dependence.

History arms:

* Complete observations and public decision notes.
* Complete observations with notes removed.
* Complete observations with another model's portable notes.
* A correct sufficient state and belief summary, only in a diagnostic arm.
* Latest observations only, as a history-ablation diagnostic.

The correct-summary arm is especially informative. If a lead disappears, the
advantage may be in reconstructing and maintaining the situation. If it
persists, it extends to planning from a shared sufficient description.

A fresh call with the same VISIBLE transcript is not necessarily equivalent
to continuing a provider-managed conversation. Available reasoning state and
context policies can differ; current official documentation describes
controls for carrying reasoning across turns on supported models. Record
that configuration and separate a stateful product track from a normalised
public-transcript track. Do not request or expose private chain-of-thought.
An exactly replicated effective input is a replication condition, not evidence
for an extra faculty of having experienced earlier turns. [S5]

Falsification matters. Pro may not lead. It may lead because it spends more.
It may lead only in reconstruction, only under certain charters, or equally
on independent short problems. Each outcome is a substantive finding.

10. SCORING AND UNCERTAINTY

Primary measure in the small exact environment: decision quality relative to
a reference that knows the public model family and the same observation
history, but not the realised hidden state or future noise.

For charter c, let J_c be the declared trajectory value. Define:

  Q*_c(h,a) = best expected J_c after action a at history h,
              allowing future policies to depend only on later observations.
  r_c(h,a) = max_a' Q*_c(h,a') - Q*_c(h,a).

This is same-information decision regret. It evaluates decisions under the
uncertainty that existed when they were made. A clairvoyant planner can be
shown as an upper bound, but is a different reference. In larger worlds, name
an approximate reference and disclose its budget; do not call a heuristic
difference an exact optimality gap.

Report a compact scorecard:

Decision quality: charter return and same-information regret.
Distribution: the participant outcome vector and unresolved losses.
Epistemics: Brier scores for prespecified binary forecasts, plus calibration.
Inquiry: useful tests bought in time and resources wasted on irrelevant tests.
Revision: warranted changes and unwarranted persistence or reversal.
Commitments: fulfilment, authorised revision, breach, and repair.
Construction: valid later operations enabled through paid prerequisites.

Avoid collapsing everything into a single ethical-intelligence score. If a
headline scalar is needed, use the preregistered charter-conditioned primary
measure and show the other dimensions alongside it. A moral disagreement
must not silently become an error in causal reasoning.

The forecast questions and timing are fixed across models. Extra leading
questions can scaffold one model more than another. Counterfactual forecasts
can be scored in the simulator using independently sampled branches, but
must not reveal unpurchased information to contestants.

Outcome luck and decision quality are reported separately. A valuable test
can return an unhelpful answer; a reckless policy can get lucky. Neither
observation alone settles the quality of the earlier decision.

11. PUBLIC DECISION RECORD AND REFEREE

Each turn requires an executable action and a short public record, not an
extraction of hidden reasoning. A useful full-game schema is:

  action: legal operation or structured action bundle
  forecasts: probabilities for the referee's named events
  evidence_ids: the few observations bearing on the decision
  decisive_reason: a brief explanation of the decisive considerations
  unresolved_cost: the participant or concern left exposed
  revision_trigger: observable condition that would change the plan
  commitment_update: new, fulfilled, revised, or outstanding obligation

Limit public prose equally, initially about 250-400 words per decision, while
recording total computation separately. The game scores choices, forecasts,
valid construction, and later consequences. Reviewers can assess whether the
public explanation misrepresents a dilemma, but eloquence is not a proxy for
competence.

Use a programmatic referee. A narrator can render structured results into
language, but cannot alter laws, resources, stakeholder permissions, or
outcomes. Template narration is preferable for the first controlled study.

Keep realised hidden world state, seeds, evaluator-only execution access, and
future packets outside the contestant's file and tool access. The charter,
scoring definitions, constraints, and terminal rule remain public. Publishing
the generator and grading algorithm is compatible with withholding the
realised world; a contestant may know the family without knowing which member
it inhabits. Never hand the contestant private referee-state files alongside
its observation packet.

Pair models on exogenous worlds. Key random draws by event, time, location,
and cause, so an extra assay does not consume the random number that would
otherwise generate an unrelated later storm. Actions still change the
observations and effects according to the world's causal rules. Paired worlds
do not imply identical observations after different policies.

Validation targets: budgets and prerequisites; hidden-state separation;
action-dependent likelihoods; repeated-evidence provenance; appropriate
terminal liabilities; replay reproducibility; and a non-clairvoyant reference.
The initial tests are justified by concrete measurement risks, not an attempt
to certify the entire future benchmark.

12. A MANAGEABLE FIRST EMPIRICAL STUDY

First use the delivered kernel to check that the mechanics create distinct
behaviours. Scripted-policy outputs are engine demonstrations, not evidence
about LLMs. The code is deliberately small enough to inspect.

Then implement a 12-decision campaign with two hidden causal properties,
one intervention-sensitive information source, one delayed investment,
one costly commitment, two public charters, and genuine conflicting goods.

An initial model pilot could use 12 withheld world seeds per charter and two
independent model runs per seed: 48 campaigns and 576 decisions per tested
configuration. This is a planning count, not a power calculation. Use pilot
variance and a preregistered minimum relevant difference to plan a larger
confirmatory study. Do not use the pilot's favourite examples as a ranking
claim.

Reference policies: immediate-return maximiser; fixed precaution; excessive
testing; an exact same-information planner where tractable; and model
reasoning from a supplied sufficient summary. Do not promise the exact
planner will remain tractable at 24 decisions with rich institutions.

Freeze rules, prompts, budgets, score definitions, scenario generation, and
holdouts before inspecting the candidate ranking. Development worlds and
public examples must be excluded from confirmation. Include unfamiliar
mechanism combinations, label permutations, and paraphrases. Test history
use through changes in causal structure, not just harder prose.

Treat campaigns and independent scenario families as statistical units;
turns within a campaign are dependent. Use paired comparisons across worlds
and uncertainty intervals clustered over worlds or families. Repeated model
runs estimate stochastic variation; they do not create new physical worlds.
Publish failures, invalid actions, retries, excluded runs, and timeout rules.

If a model's output is invalid, use a fixed repair allowance or a declared
default action, identical for all contestants. Do not selectively rescue the
preferred model. No discretionary prompt coaching during held-out games.

13. MAKING THE RESULT LEGIBLE AND ENGAGING

Show the campaign as a timeline of short decision cards: what was known,
what was forecast, what was chosen, which cost remained, and what happened
later. Highlight the earlier observation or promise that makes a late
decision intelligible. A spectator should be able to see the causal link.

Reveal hidden state only after play. Before each consequential reveal,
invite the audience to predict an event or choose a course. The drama comes
from watching a defensible early choice produce a new, difficult situation,
then seeing whether the player understands what changed.

Use aggregate results plus paired examples selected through a published
rule, such as the median paired difference, a common failure mode, and a
case where the leading system loses. A handpicked highlight can be shown
if labelled illustrative. Do not use it to imply an aggregate advantage.

Avoid a cartoon moral leaderboard. A useful display puts decision regret,
forecast calibration, expenditure, and each community's outcomes next to
one another, with the active charter clearly visible.

Later settings can preserve the same mechanisms while changing the story:
a research institute choosing costly experiments under conflicting public
obligations; an expedition managing a shared life-support system; or an
energy network with incompatible reliability, access, and land concerns.
Cross-domain transfer tests whether the acquired relation survives a new
surface story. Multiplayer bargaining is a later track because strategic
communication adds another source of variance to the initial capability test.

14. SIGNIFICANT INSIGHTS AND NEXT INVESTIGATION

First, the cleanest failure may be trusting evidence made uninformative by
one's own intervention. This joins causal reasoning and long-term context
without depending on deception. It is implementable in a tiny exact game.

Second, acquiring useful knowledge can change the moral problem. An audit
may reveal who bears an invisible burden, altering what a published charter
requires. Better reasoning need not always produce a more comfortable choice.

Third, coherence can require institutional replacement. A concern can persist
while its carrier changes. A policy that merely preserves the original
organisation may fail the continuing mandate.

Fourth, reversal does not erase history. Refilling a reservoir does not restore
lost harvests or undo commitments. A correct state representation must retain
the consequences that the headline indicator hides.

Fifth, useful inquiry is conditional on future action. More measurements are
not necessarily better, and temporary ignorance can be a rational consequence
of scarcity. Costs, deadlines, and feasible responses give inquiry its value.

Sixth, the ability to construct later options can be assessed without treating
more options as an absolute good. Test whether the new operation can actually
be used, by whom, at what cost, and under what mandate.

Seventh, public notes can make accumulated understanding transferable. If
another model benefits from a good dossier, some of the apparent continuity
resides in an external representation. That is a finding about the mechanism
of competent performance, not a reason to dismiss it.

The first explanatory question should be:

  As the interval grows between acquiring a causal distinction and needing
  it again, which tested systems still use it correctly after their own
  interventions have changed its evidence, obligations, and possible uses?

Run that comparison with matched histories, explicit charters, and reported
resource use. It directly tests the proposed sustained-reasoning capability.
It is sharper than asking which model writes the most convincing account
of a difficult moral situation.

15. WHAT WAS IMPLEMENTED AND EXECUTED

The accompanying Python kernel implements four seasons and eight submitted
decisions. It contains a binary persistent hidden condition, a surface sensor
whose likelihood depends on the previous operation, a paid upstream assay,
five possible operations with prerequisites, an investment that enables reuse,
and a public terminal liability. The exact reference enumerates future
observations and actions under the public world family. It does not receive
the actual hidden condition or seed.

Fourteen semantic tests passed. They check action-conditioned updating,
information-price changes, irrelevant inquiry, a hand-calculated one-season
value, charter and horizon sensitivity, public/private separation, keyed
randomness, budgets, construction, and faithful save/resume.

The saved scripted demonstration covers 64 paired world seeds and four
policies: 256 games in total. No LLM was evaluated. Mean results are:

Policy                 Realised charter loss   Local excess loss   Brier
Exact continuation      8.74609                 0.00000             0.05039
Always assay           10.13672                 1.28260             0.03855
Myopic                  9.64844                 1.17788             0.11919
Ignore blending         8.74609                 0.00000             0.05039

Lower is better in each column, under its own definition. These figures are
descriptive, not model comparisons or confidence-qualified general claims.
The always-assay policy obtains more accurate forecasts while paying for
worse decisions under the declared charter. Forecasts and consequential
judgment therefore need separate reporting.

The default complete-run paths do not distinguish the exact policy from the
policy that ignores blending. This limitation is recorded; matching scores
cannot be interpreted as a successful test of that error.

A separately supplied hypothetical checkpoint (three seasons remaining,
four credits, hazard belief 0.4) verifies inquiry-price sensitivity. Expected
remaining loss without an assay is 9.0; buying an assay yields 8.27262 when
it costs one credit and 9.67210 when it costs two. Thus the exact choice
changes with the real opportunity cost. This is an isolated diagnostic, not
a checkpoint sampled from the default initial sensor history.

A further reachable checkpoint was generated using legal choices none,
blend, none in seed 29. It was selected by searching seeds 0-49 for the first
action contrast and is explicitly illustrative. An initial unblended clear
reading gives hazard belief 4/31. A later warning after blending leaves that
belief unchanged, whereas ignoring blending raises it incorrectly to 32/59.
The correct reference chooses pump; the mistaken interpretation chooses blend.
The excess expected loss is small, about 0.08505 toy units. This demonstrates
that misreading intervention-dependent evidence can produce excess precaution
as well as false reassurance. It is not a held-out performance result.

The kernel omits consent, negotiation, changing institutions, changing values,
multiple hidden mechanisms, inquiry delays, and the long full campaign.
Those are design specifications in this report, not implemented capabilities.
The code, rules, tests, full traces, and checkpoint are in this repository.
